## AI is the best tutor you've ever had, if you use it right

You have something learners ten years ago didn't: a patient expert you can ask anything, at any hour. Used well, that's an enormous advantage. Used badly, it quietly stops you from learning at all.

The difference comes down to one question: **who is doing the thinking?**

## The trap

When an AI hands you a finished answer, you read it, it makes sense, and you feel like you understand. That feeling is misleading. Following someone else's reasoning is far easier than producing it yourself, so your brain mistakes "I recognize this" for "I can do this".

The test is simple. Close the answer and try again tomorrow. If you can't, you didn't learn it. You watched it.

<Callout kind="warn" title="The sign you've fallen into the trap">
You're finishing exercises quickly but couldn't explain your own code to a friend.
</Callout>

## The routine

Use this every time you're stuck:

1. **Try it yourself first.** Even a wrong attempt helps, because it shows you exactly where your understanding runs out.
2. **Ask for a hint, not the answer.** Show the AI what you tried and the exact error, then ask what to look at.
3. **Finish it yourself.** Take the hint and keep going.
4. **Explain it back.** Ask the AI to quiz you, or explain the solution in your own words. If you can't, you're not done.

Groundwork is built the same way. Every lesson asks you to **predict** before you learn, **do** something real, and **recall** it from memory afterwards. That's on purpose.

## When to let AI just do it

This isn't about refusing help. Working consultants use AI constantly. The rule is:

> **AI does the parts you're not trying to learn. You do the parts you are.**

Typing out 50 rows of test data? Let AI do it. Writing your first loop? Do it yourself.

## Setting up your tutor

Most AI tools let you give standing instructions, or you can paste some at the start of a chat. Something like this works well:

```text
I'm a beginner learning to code. Act as my tutor, not my ghostwriter.
When I'm stuck, ask what I've tried and give me the smallest useful hint.
Only give the full answer if I ask twice. Afterwards, quiz me with one
or two questions. If you're unsure about something, say so and point me
to the official documentation.
```
